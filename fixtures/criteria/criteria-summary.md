# Public level-of-care criteria summary

A short paraphrase of two public frameworks for the Payer Policy agent. It is not the official text. Plans often use proprietary criteria (InterQual, MCG) that we can't use; this summary stands in for them.

## ASAM Criteria (substance use)

Six dimensions are assessed together to match a patient to a level of care. Names below follow the widely cited 3rd edition; the 4th edition (2023) regroups some of them.

| # | Dimension | What a reviewer looks for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Acute intoxication and withdrawal potential | Withdrawal severity and scores (CIWA, COWS), history of seizures or DTs, need for medical withdrawal management |
| 2 | Biomedical conditions and complications | Medical problems needing monitoring or complicating withdrawal |
| 3 | Emotional, behavioral or cognitive conditions | Co-occurring psychiatric symptoms, suicide risk, cognitive problems |
| 4 | Readiness to change | Motivation and engagement in treatment |
| 5 | Relapse, continued use or continued problem potential | Relapse history, failed lower levels of care, coping skills |
| 6 | Recovery or living environment | Safety and support at home, substance use in the home, housing |

Levels of care (simplified): 1 outpatient; 2.1 intensive outpatient (IOP); 2.5 partial hospitalization (PHP); 3.1 to 3.7 residential (clinically managed to medically monitored); 4 medically managed intensive inpatient. Withdrawal management can be provided at several levels; inpatient withdrawal management generally requires significant withdrawal risk or medical or psychiatric complications.

## LOCUS (mental health)

Six dimensions (the fourth has two parts) are scored, and the total plus specific rules point to a level of care.

| # | Dimension | What a reviewer looks for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Risk of harm | Suicidal or homicidal ideation, plan, intent, self-harm, ability to stay safe |
| II | Functional status | Ability to care for self, work, relate to others |
| III | Medical, addictive and psychiatric co-morbidity | Other conditions that complicate treatment |
| IV-A | Recovery environment: level of stress | Stressors at home, work or in the community |
| IV-B | Recovery environment: level of support | Family, housing and services available |
| V | Treatment and recovery history | Response to past treatment, failures at lower levels |
| VI | Engagement and recovery status | Understanding of illness, participation in treatment |

Levels of care: 0 basic services; 1 recovery maintenance; 2 low-intensity community-based; 3 high-intensity community-based; 4 medically monitored non-residential (e.g., PHP/IOP); 5 medically monitored residential; 6 medically managed residential (inpatient).

## Questions every reviewer asks

1. Could the patient be treated safely at a lower level of care?
2. Is each dimension supported by documentation?
3. Is there a discharge plan with a target date and step-down?
4. Do the intake, treatment plan and notes tell the same story?

Sources: ASAM, *The ASAM Criteria*; AACP, *LOCUS*. Consult the official publications for actual use.
